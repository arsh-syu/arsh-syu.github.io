Walnut Exam Module

Run every examination in one place, from the question paper to signed results.

The Walnut Exam Module is Walnut’s examination platform for universities and institutions, part of Walnut LMS; the Online Proctored Examinations service runs on it. Set papers with your own teams, release them securely, supervise candidates with AI-assisted proctoring and a human proctor, then evaluate, tabulate and publish digitally signed results, so any change to them can be detected.

How it works

From the question paper to the published result.

Five steps, in one platform, with a person able to step in at every stage.

  1. 01

    Plan the examination

    Register subjects and their syllabus, schedule each examination and issue admit cards.

  2. 02

    Set the paper

    Build a blueprint, then setters write questions in a question bank tagged to Bloom’s levels, reviewed by a Head Examiner and moderators.

  3. 03

    Release it securely

    Question sets A, B and C are sealed. A set is released only when two controllers have signed for it.

  4. 04

    Supervise the exam

    Candidates pass a system check and an identity check-in; a proctor can issue an entry code to a candidate who can’t sign in. AI-assisted proctoring raises flags and warnings for a human proctor to act on.

  5. 05

    Evaluate and publish

    Evaluation and moderation lead to tabulated results, published signed. Candidates can challenge a question or ask for re-evaluation.

For the Controller of Examinations

One command view of every examination, before, during and after.

  • A subject and syllabus registry, exam registration and scheduling, and admit cards.
  • A blueprint and question setting by teams of setters, a Head Examiner and moderators.
  • Question sets A, B and C, released only with two controller signatures.
  • Live oversight on exam day, with candidate access and proctor reports.
  • Result tabulation and signed publishing, with question challenges and re-evaluation.

For question setters

Write better papers faster, from your own material.

  • A question bank of many question types, each tagged to Bloom’s levels, with bulk upload, answer keys and rubrics.
  • An AI question generator, powered by Anthropic’s Claude, that drafts questions from your institution’s own study material.
  • Every AI-drafted question is saved as a draft for a person to check before it’s submitted.
  • Tasks, answer keys and an evaluation queue in the same place.
Try it in the demo

For proctors

See every candidate, and act on what matters.

  • A live console with each candidate’s status, warnings and access requests.
  • AI-assisted flags for an absent face, more than one person, phones and books, looking away, voices and leaving full screen or the exam window.
  • Escalations that need a person, and an incident log of every decision.
  • Automatic termination is off by default: a third warning asks the proctor to confirm.
Try it in the demo

For results

From final marks to published results, with nothing changed on the way.

  • Evaluation and moderation, then tabulation of the final marks.
  • Publishing checks the figures again and refuses if anything has changed.
  • Digitally signed results, so any change can be detected; corrections are linked revisions.
  • Candidates can challenge a question or ask for re-evaluation, and practise first with a mock test.
Try it in the demo

Integrity

Results you can stand behind.

Every step leaves a record, and a change to the record shows.

  1. 01

    Two signatures to release a paper

    Question sets stay sealed until two controllers have signed. A sealed set can’t be released on one person’s signature.

  2. 02

    A human makes the call

    AI-assisted proctoring watches for an absent face, more than one person, phones and books, looking away, voices and leaving the exam window. It flags and warns, and a proctor decides whether to end an exam: automatic removal is off by default.

  3. 03

    Signed results

    Published results are digitally signed, so any change to them can be detected. A correction is published as a new, linked revision.

  4. 04

    A tamper-evident audit trail

    Each entry in the audit trail is linked to the one before it, so a change to the record shows.
