    Walnut Proctored Exam Module

    Secure, live-proctored online exams.

    How Walnut runs and monitors every online examination, from the candidate wall to the final answer. This is the module as it runs today for a state university’s online and distance programmes: trained proctors, automatic integrity checks and a guided exam screen, operated end to end by Walnut.

    For institutions that examine online, and the students who sit the paper

    Before exam day: how the paper is set

    For the Controller of Examinations and question setters

    Your own teams set the paper, and it stays sealed until two controllers have signed for it.

    1. Step 1Plan the examinationRegister subjects and their syllabus, schedule each examination and issue admit cards.
    2. Step 2Build the blueprintAn examination blueprint for each paper, with AI extraction of modules, units and topics from an uploaded syllabus.
    3. Step 3Tag to Bloom’s taxonomySetters write questions in a question bank of many question types, each tagged to Bloom’s levels, with bulk upload, answer keys and rubrics.
    4. Step 4Draft with AI, check by handAn AI question generator drafts questions from your institution’s own study material. Every draft is saved for a person to check before it is submitted.
    5. Step 5Review and moderateA Head Examiner and moderators review the questions the setters write.
    6. Step 6Encrypt, seal and releaseQuestion sets A, B and C are encrypted and sealed. A set is released only when two controllers have signed for it.

    Before the paper: how a student gets in

    For the students who sit the paper

    Every candidate is checked before the paper opens, so the right person sits it.

    1. Step 1System checkThe candidate signs in, passes a system check and allows the camera when asked.
    2. Step 2Aadhaar verificationA live Aadhaar check, by eKYC or document capture.
    3. Step 3Live face matchAn identity check-in on camera: the face is matched before entry.
    4. Step 4Verification lobbyDocument and AI checks in a verification lobby. A candidate who cannot enter automatically asks the proctor, who checks the camera and approves or rejects, or issues an entry code.
    5. Step 5The paper opensA guided exam screen, with liveness checks during the examination.

    During the paper: what the proctor and the student see

    Every candidate, on one console.

    Live counts
    Assigned, connected, not connected, submitted, warnings and pending access, at the top of the console.
    Candidate wall
    A live camera tile for every connected candidate. A tile turns dark when a camera is off and a Break label shows who is paused.
    Assigned roster
    Each candidate’s status and exam controls, with a one-click Resume Exam for a paused candidate.
    Camera, screen and mic
    Select a candidate to open the session: the webcam on the left, the entire screen on the right, and the microphone, all live, so the proctor sees exactly what they are doing.

    AI flags; proctors decide.

    Proctoring alerts
    Another person detected, candidate not present, left the exam window, looking away from the screen. Each is flagged to the proctor and shown to the candidate with the reason and a running warning count.
    Integrity trust score
    A live score out of 100 with every deduction listed: face not consistently visible, left the exam window and for how long. Deductions are proportionate; a small issue costs a little, nothing is an automatic penalty.
    Violation log
    Every event time-stamped with a snapshot, kept for review.
    Review threshold
    When the score falls below the threshold the session is marked for review. A proctor makes the final decision, never the AI alone.

    Time, breaks, access and resumes.

    Add minutes, break, terminate
    For every candidate in the roster: extra minutes for genuine delays, a Break that locks the exam screen while monitoring stays connected, and Terminate in serious cases.
    Manual access requests
    A candidate who cannot enter automatically asks the proctor, who checks the camera and chooses Approve or Reject.
    Resume with a remark
    A session closed for a violation can be resumed if the proctor finds it justified. Every resume needs a written reason, so there is an audit trail of who resumed which exam and why.
    Status at a glance
    Not started, in process, on break, submitted, closed due to violation.

    Private chat, in real time.

    One private chat per attempt
    Proctors and candidates talk without disturbing the rest of the batch.
    Clear, polite guidance
    “Dear student, kindly focus on camera.” “Speaking in the exam is not allowed.”
    Help with problems
    “Kindly use a stable internet connection and re-login.” Access and connection issues are sorted in the chat.
    Questions answered
    Students can ask about their score and their warnings, and get an answer during the exam.

    What candidates see.

    A guided exam screen
    Sections for multiple-choice and descriptive answers with progress, the time left and the student helpline at the top, and a small self view so candidates know the session is recorded.
    A reason for every answer
    One question at a time. Each multiple-choice answer comes with a short written reason, which helps confirm genuine understanding.
    Question palette
    Answered, marked for review and not visited, colour-coded by section.
    Descriptive answers
    A rich-text editor for long answers, with an optional PDF or photo of working uploaded within a short time window.

    Behind the scenes

    Trained proctors supervise every live batch, each with an assigned set of candidates, while a shared wall display gives the whole team one view of the examination. AI flags what it sees; a person decides.

    After the paper: evaluation and results

    For exam coordinators, evaluators and the Controller of Examinations

    Objective answers are auto-marked; only the rest need a human.

    Allocation policy

    The exam coordinator chooses how answer scripts are distributed, then hands them to evaluators. Six ways:

    • Whole paperOne candidate’s entire script goes to one evaluator.
    • Question-wiseEach question is marked by one evaluator across every script.
    • Question creatorEach answer is routed to the faculty who authored that question.
    • Round robinScripts are dealt out in rotation for an even count each.
    • Equal workloadBalanced by estimated marking effort rather than script count.
    • ManualThe coordinator assigns every script by hand.

    The evaluator pool: question setters, moderators, evaluators and the Head Examiner.

    From final marks to published results

    Evaluate, moderate, tabulate
    Evaluation and moderation, then tabulation of the final marks.
    Checked again at publishing
    Publishing checks the figures again and refuses if anything has changed.
    Digitally signed results
    Any change to a published result can be detected. A correction is published as a new, linked revision.
    Challenges and re-evaluation
    Candidates can challenge a question or ask for re-evaluation.
    A tamper-evident audit trail
    Each entry in the audit trail is linked to the one before it, so a change to the record shows.

    Grievances: raised, reviewed and closed

    For students, exam coordinators and faculty

    A student can question a question or a mark. Each grievance is routed by rule, decided with a reason and tracked to its close.

    During the exam

    Question grievance

    1. 1The student reports a questionFrom the exam screen, while the paper is on.
    2. 2The coordinator sees them togetherAll grievances in one view, by question.
    3. 3An expert decidesRouted to the faculty, the chairman or a subject expert, who decides if it is valid.
    4. 4Action is takenChange the answer key, drop the question or give the marks.

    After the result

    Marks grievance

    1. 1The student raises a grievanceAfter viewing the evaluated paper, and tracks its status.
    2. 2The coordinator assigns itTo the faculty or the evaluator.
    3. 3The faculty respondsWith a reason and a decision. Re-evaluation can go to a different evaluator, with an optional blind review.
    4. 4Changed or closedIf the marks change, the coordinator reviews and approves. If not, the grievance is closed.

    Closure: every grievance closed and the final marks verified, then the result is locked and the final result is published.

    Frequently asked questions

    Before you get in touch.

    Something we haven’t answered? Ask it in the form and a consultant replies.

    What does Walnut Data Tech do?

    Technology and services to launch, run and grow online programmes: 11 service areas in four stages. Set up (infrastructure, content and Walnut LMS), enrol (marketing, counselling and admissions), run (proctored examinations, student support, reporting and automation) and outcomes (apprenticeship and placement assistance).

    Do we have to take everything?

    No. You can start from the complete stack or choose only the service areas you need. The five apps inside Walnut LMS can also be taken one at a time, as a bundle or all together.

    How is it priced?

    In one of two ways: revenue sharing, where each service is priced as a percentage of programme revenue, or a one-time payment, where each service has a fixed price agreed up front. The price of each service is shared in your proposal.

    What happens after we send the form?

    A Walnut consultant gets in touch to understand what you need, shows you Walnut LMS with sample data from your kind of programme, and sends a proposal for the service areas you choose.

    Does Walnut LMS follow the UGC’s requirements?

    Every course is organised in the UGC four quadrants: e-tutorial, e-content, web resources and discussion, and self-assessment. Digital content is delivered through the LMS and mobile app in accordance with UGC Regulations, and proctored examinations are conducted as per prevailing UGC Regulations.

    Do you help with UGC/DEB and Academic Bank of Credit reporting?

    Yes. Walnut prepares institutional reports on admissions, fees collection, student backlogs, examination data and academic records as required by the University and regulatory authorities including UGC/DEB, and supports registration and data sharing with the Academic Bank of Credit.

    How are online examinations proctored?

    With AI proctoring, human proctoring, or both together, chosen for each examination. Trained proctors supervise every live batch, each with an assigned set of candidates. AI flags what it sees; a proctor makes the final decision, never the AI alone.

    How do you know the right person is sitting the paper?

    Through a live Aadhaar check (eKYC or document capture), a face match before entry, a verification lobby and liveness checks during the examination.

    Can questions be set from our own syllabus?

    Yes. Questions are generated from the uploaded syllabus with AI, added manually, uploaded in bulk or digitised from the University’s question banks, in nine question types, and reviewed by faculty. A paper is released only after two-step verification.

    Where is our data stored?

    In accordance with the University’s requirements and mutually agreed terms.

    Is Walnut LMS only for universities?

    No. Colleges and institutes get batch management with faculty assigned per course, a faculty approval workflow for study material, self-directed courses and certificates anyone can verify by number.

    Can the LMS carry our own branding?

    Yes. A super admin runs many institutions on one platform, each with its own logo, colours, roles and settings.

    What does a school use?

    On-screen evaluation of scanned answer sheets, an improvement plan for every student and teacher, CBSE-aligned lesson design on the NCERT curriculum map, and attendance, assignments and progress that parents can follow.

    Can we start with one app?

    Yes. Each app works fully on its own, so a school can start with one and add the others later.

    I am a student. How do I get Walnut LMS?

    Through your university, college, school or institute, which gives you a student login. Behind it are your courses, recorded and live lectures, notes and e-books, assignments, self-assessment, attendance, results and fees.

    What will I see in a proctored examination?

    Sections for multiple-choice and descriptive answers with progress, the time left and the student helpline at the top, and a small self view so candidates know the session is recorded. Question palette: answered, marked for review and not visited, colour-coded by section.

    Can anyone check my certificate?

    Yes. Certificates are verified on Walnut LMS, by their number.
